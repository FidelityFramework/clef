## Disagreements resolved in the recommendations

Independent review produced useful errors as well as useful challenges. The curated conclusions deliberately do not repeat these overclaims:

1. **Existing rules veto alternatives.** The first Fable review rejected subtyping largely because today's boundary forbids it. Its continuation withdrew that veto. Current source contracts identify changes to make explicit. They cannot decide the research against those changes.
2. **Canonicality establishes principality.** Both a specialized solution and a principal solution can have canonical syntax. Checking one substitution proves only its stated instance. The continuation corrected this. Chapter 4 requires the actual solution/generalization argument.
3. **Higher rank cures polymorphic mutation.** Separate callback, scope-escape and shared mutable-storage tests are required.
4. **Subtyping is inherently weak for native measures.** A heterogeneous measured union can be intentional sum-like data. Rejecting incompatible arithmetic at use may remain sound. Native layout/no-boxing requirements do not alone forbid checked structural views.
5. **Overload alternatives preclude principal qualified schemes.** A scheme may retain constraints. The actual calculus must decide entailment, coherence and ambiguity.
6. **One machinery means one algebra, or several algebras prove one algorithm impossible.** Neither follows. A common implementation can orchestrate different theories. Each claimed combined property still needs justification.
7. **NFT already replaces subtype inference.** Its restoration/completion rules do not by themselves define structural joins, subsumption or variance. The proposed adapter correspondence remains unproved. Conversely, the review does not claim no future NFT-informed calculus could subsume those judgments.
8. **A BigInt carrier or a nine-bit capacity requirement is an executed realization result.** Carrier capacity, checked application overflow policy and actual emitted/library behavior are separate evidence.
9. **Width and Mealy/Moore reasoning are FPGA-only.** The normative width contract explicitly includes other targets. The FPGA example exposes per-field representation and output dependencies. It does not change the source scheme or prove every backend supports every requested realization.
10. **NFT must replace subtyping to deserve central investigation.** Its own elaboration, equality, identity and demand rules are immediate H1 subjects. A general adapter/subtype correspondence remains a distinct H3 hypothesis. The finite reciprocal core does not automatically admit resource-bearing rows or opaque mutable foreign handles.
11. **Foreign type elicitation proves foreign behavior.** Declaration/layout evidence contributes boundary premises. It does not establish a C algorithm, arbitrary callback behavior or a release merely because ownership metadata is present.
12. **A quieter graph proves faster compilation.** Count semantic changes and total inference/checking cost while testing dependency completeness against full recomputation. Merging effectful calls or live identities is an invalid shortcut, and a richer sound analysis can legitimately cost more.

Sage's follow-up also misattributed the restoration example to the video. It comes from NFT. Its informal observation-profile condition was a proposal, not a formal translation theorem. Raw generated rankings were therefore not promoted into the horizon recommendations.
